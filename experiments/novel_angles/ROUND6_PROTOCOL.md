# Round 6: robustness and exploratory follow-ups

This is a retrospective robustness analysis of the known Round 5 results at `e74a258`, not a blinded preregistration or a new replication. Freeze these additional analyses before computing their outcomes. Do not change the earlier scores, eligibility, decision rule, or gates.

1. **Multiplicity.** Use all 88 two-sided model-by-cohort pairing EER contrasts. The saved files have percentile endpoints but not draws, so approximate standard error as interval width divided by twice the 97.5th standard normal quantile, and compute normal-reference p-values from the unrounded point divided by this error. Apply Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) at .05 across the same family. Report both; these are approximate sensitivity results, not exact bootstrap tests. BH's dependence assumptions have not been established for shared scores and cohorts; prefer Holm for familywise interpretation.
2. **Splits and information flow.** Rebuild the exact Round 5 identity groups from the prepared inputs, assert all four roles are disjoint and exhaustive in all 20 rotations per cohort, and count each identity's role assignments. Trace the full Round 5 sources at enrollment, normalization, encoding, ensemble fitting and cutoff. In the targeted rerun, perturb late scores before recalculating operating decisions and require both calibration-visit ledgers and late cutoff columns to remain identical. Exercise the actual pre-fit slicing and normalization, including reference-only cosine normalization, with a late-data perturbation test. Separate source and intervention checks from what was recorded by the original batch run.
3. **False-accept precision.** For paired cosine, report actual counted claimant × impostor attempts, observed accepts, unique held-out impostors and identities ever accepted. Use one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson bounds for the optimistic independent-attempt scenario and for the event of at least one acceptance per impostor in this design. Neither guarantees independence (identities share claimants, fits and thresholds); the events are not the same estimand. Also report the 95th percentile of the Round 5 crossed-cluster bootstrap (same people resampled for claimant and impostor roles) for the per-attempt FAR; retain the original two-sided interval for gate comparisons. Find minimum independent trial counts at each observed error count, holding errors fixed (not a recruitment forecast). Report the zero-event best case and compare feasible identity counts; do not convert repeated attempts into independent people.
4. **Probe budget and seeds.** Reuse prepared FIFs and score only cosine, logistic regression and tangent-SVM (one fixed template method and the main-cohort lowest EER methods). Use first 8/16/24 retained windows per state, or 2/4/6 for WBCIC-SHU. Keep enrollment and learned transformations fixed at Round 5 settings; scores use the corresponding time budget and recalibrate each budget only with calibration identities. Report paired EER deltas and original gates at every budget; the largest budget must reproduce Round 5 EER, FRR, FAR, and pairing intervals. Reuse recorded seeds 42/43 for deep-learning and stochastic ensemble results: show each cohort's paired EER change across seeds by row, as descriptive sensitivity, not a stability estimate.
5. **Heterogeneity.** For the three targeted methods at the largest budget, give the strict-improvement/tie/worsening count for the individual claimant's EER against its retained held-out impostors and FRR at its frozen cutoff. Also recompute the pooled EER contrast after deleting each subject from both claimant and impostor roles with models and cutoffs fixed. Report only aggregated counts/ranges, not identities. Subject-level EER and the pooled EER describe different comparisons.
6. **New ideas.** At the largest budget only, test an equal average of cosine and logistic scores after separate empirical percentile transforms learned from calibration impostors for each claimant and arm. Recalibrate the fused cutoff on those same calibration identities; no weights or choices learned from final data. Compare fused paired EER and frozen FRR/FAR with both constituents using the shared bootstrap. Report ties caused by the finite empirical transforms. Independently pool cosine's four known EER deltas with inverse-variance DerSimonian–Laird random effects and report Q, I², and a modified Hartung–Knapp 95% interval (scale at least one; t with three degrees of freedom). This is a descriptive summary across unlike protocols, with the approximate standard errors from step 1. Do not interpret it as an independent full-duration replication.
